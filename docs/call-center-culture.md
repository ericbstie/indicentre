# Call-centre culture for the satire

One page of real details to use as lines and props. The satire targets the office (quotas, fines, euphemisms, tiny prizes), never the people or where they're from. Read with the slop rules in [consistency.md](consistency.md).

## What real scam floors look like (journalism, leaks, raids)

- **Fines on a printed list.** The leaked chats of a scam compound in Laos (WIRED, 2025) list fines in yuan: 50 for starting no "first chat" that day, 200 for sleeping at the desk or watching videos, 500 for sleeping late, 1,000 for a false progress report. If you refuse to sign the form admitting it, the fine doubles. Base pay was 3,500 a month, and the fines took most of it.
- **The daily report template.** Every worker filed the same form each day: team, name, active accounts, chats started, chats ongoing, successes, monthly target. Managers corrected small details in the fake personas, like which car the persona drives.
- **Euphemisms.** A victim was "a customer". A first theft was "opening a customer" and a repeat theft on the same person was a "recharge". The Thane call centres (Reuters, 2016) called the same work "customer care".
- **The drum.** In the compound, big wins were celebrated by striking a ceremonial drum, and the boss's announcement got 26 thumbs-up and salute emoji. Boiler rooms ring a gong or a bell.
- **Motivational posts.** "Don't fear the fine. Let it fuel your fire." Also: "Don't resist the company's rules and regulations."
- **Small prizes.** Thane managers handed out weekly cash or gadgets for hitting targets. One caller got 2 rupees for every dollar taken. One trainee said she took the job because "people aren't hiring".
- **Week one is memorising.** New hires spent their first week memorising pages of dialogue. Recorded calls were kept for training. Pens and personal phones weren't allowed on the floor.
- **Night shifts on the victims' clock.** Shifts ran about 15 hours (11:30 pm to 2 pm), with quiet hours in the dorm afterwards. Boiler rooms stay open until 4 am for the same reason.
- **End-of-month crunch.** A boiler-room insider told the Bureau of Investigative Journalism that most of the fraud happens in the last ten days of the month, when bonus targets are close. Retention desks were fired if they missed a monthly figure.
- **It looks like any office.** A scambaiter's CCTV leak (Malwarebytes, 2020) found nothing visually unusual: rows of desks, headsets, a manager walking the aisles. That ordinariness is the joke to play.

## How the good satire does it

- **Glengarry Glen Ross:** the bottom two get fired, there's a ladder of prizes (first prize a car, second a set of steak knives), the "good leads" are locked away for closers only, and "coffee is for closers". The cruelty sits in a ranked list and a prize table.
- **Office Space:** a cover sheet on every report, the memo you got three times, "come in on Saturday", the minimum number of pieces of flair. Bosses are polite, precise and tireless.
- **Severance:** rewards out of all proportion to the work (a waffle party, finger traps, an eraser), and a handbook quoted like scripture.
- **The Office:** home-made awards, the "World's Best Boss" mug the boss bought himself, and meetings about meetings.
- **What these have in common:** one exact number, one tiny prize, a boss who believes the poster. Nobody explains the joke.

## Usable props and lines (made up, grounded in the above)

- The fines list taped by the printer. Fines that double if you don't sign the form.
- The drum (or a bell expensed by the boss) for any win over a set amount.
- The end-of-day form with its fields. "Chats started: 0" is its own punishment.
- Weekly prizes: a desk fan, a phone charger, the good chair, ten minutes earlier on Friday.
- Recordings of good calls kept "for the new hires".
- Leads graded like produce, with the good ones in a locked drawer.
- Posters: an eagle, a mountain, "Don't fear the fine".
- No pens on the floor. The thumbs-up reply chain to the boss's group message.

## What to avoid

- **Ethnic caricature.** No accents written phonetically, no jokes about names, food or religion, no "Indian call centre" framing in game text. Much real-world scam labour is trafficked and forced (UN, 2023). The boss and the system are the target, never the callers' nationality.
- **Real names.** No real firms, call centres, scambaiters, police operations or news outlets in game text. Sources stay in this note.
- **How-to.** No working scripts, gift-card instructions, payment rails, tools or spoofing method. The details above are about the _workplace_: fines, prizes, forms, posters. A line may say that a script exists, not what it says.
- **Glamour.** Leave out the boiler-room drugs and parties. They make the job look fun, and the game's point is that it isn't.
